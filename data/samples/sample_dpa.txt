SAMPLE PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION ACT, 2020

CHAPTER I — PRELIMINARY

Section 1. Short title and commencement.
(1) This Act may be cited as the Personal Data Protection Act, 2020.
(2) It shall come into force on such date as the Minister may, by notification
in the Gazette, appoint.

Section 2. Definitions.
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—
(a) "personal data" means data, whether true or not, about an individual who
    can be identified from that data, or from that data and other information
    to which the organisation has or is likely to have access;
(b) "controller" means a person who, alone or jointly with others, determines
    the purposes and means of the processing of personal data;
(c) "Commissioner" means the Data Protection Commissioner appointed under
    section 30;
(d) "data subject" means an identified or identifiable natural person.

CHAPTER II — GENERAL OBLIGATIONS

Section 5. Lawful basis for processing.
An organisation shall only process personal data with the consent of the data
subject or on another lawful basis specified in section 6.

Section 6. Lawful bases other than consent.
Processing is lawful where it is necessary—
(a) for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party;
(b) for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;
(c) in order to protect the vital interests of the data subject;
(d) for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest.

Section 8. Notification of personal data breach.
(1) A controller shall notify the Commissioner without undue delay, and in any
    event within 72 hours, of any personal data breach, and shall notify
    affected data subjects where the breach is likely to result in a high risk
    to their rights.
(2) The notification shall describe the nature of the breach, the categories
    and approximate number of data subjects affected, and the measures taken
    or proposed to address the breach.

CHAPTER III — DATA SUBJECT RIGHTS

Section 12. Right of access.
A data subject has the right to obtain from the controller confirmation as to
whether personal data concerning them is being processed, and where that is
the case, access to the personal data.

Section 13. Right to rectification.
A data subject has the right to obtain from the controller without undue
delay the rectification of inaccurate personal data concerning them.

Section 14. Right to erasure.
A data subject has the right to obtain from the controller the erasure of
personal data concerning them without undue delay where one of the grounds in
subsection (2) applies.

CHAPTER IV — CROSS-BORDER TRANSFER AND LOCALISATION

Section 25. Localisation of personal data of citizens.
Personal data of citizens shall be stored on servers located within the
territory of the State. Sectoral regulators may permit copy-on-shore exceptions
where strictly necessary and subject to safeguards.

Section 26. Transfer of personal data outside the country.
(1) An organisation shall not transfer any personal data outside the country
    unless it has taken appropriate steps to ensure that the recipient is
    bound by legally enforceable obligations to provide to the transferred
    personal data a standard of protection that is comparable to the
    protection under this Act.
(2) Subsection (1) does not apply where—
    (a) the transfer is necessary for the conclusion or performance of a
        contract between the data subject and the controller;
    (b) the data subject has given explicit consent to the proposed transfer
        after being informed of the possible risks; or
    (c) the transfer is necessary on important public interest grounds
        recognised by law.
(3) The Commissioner may issue guidelines specifying jurisdictions that
    provide a comparable level of protection for the purposes of subsection
    (1), and may revise the list from time to time.

CHAPTER V — THE DATA PROTECTION COMMISSION

Section 30. Establishment of the Commission.
There is hereby established a body to be known as the Data Protection
Commission.

Section 31. Independence of the Commission.
The Data Protection Commission shall be independent in the exercise of its
functions and shall not be subject to the direction or control of any person
or authority.

CHAPTER VI — ENFORCEMENT

Section 40. Administrative fines.
The Commissioner may impose administrative fines for contraventions of this
Act, not exceeding ten million units of currency or two per cent of annual
turnover, whichever is higher.
